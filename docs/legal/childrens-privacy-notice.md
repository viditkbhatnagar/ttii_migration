# Children's Privacy Notice

**Last reviewed:** {{LAST_REVIEW_DATE}} | **Next review:** {{NEXT_REVIEW_DATE}}

> A "child" for this notice means a person under 18 years of age, in line with §2(f) of the **Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023** ("DPDP Act"). DPDP §9 places special obligations on a Data Fiduciary that processes children's personal data. This notice explains how {{LEGAL_ENTITY_NAME}} ("TTII") meets those obligations.

This notice is read together with our [Privacy Policy](privacy-policy.md). Where there is a conflict between this notice and the Privacy Policy in respect of a child, **this notice prevails**.

## 1. Who this applies to

TTII delivers programmes to a mixed audience. Some programmes are for adult learners (teachers in training); others are delivered through partner schools or centres in which children may enrol.

| Programme type | Age targeting | Children's participation |
|---|---|---|
| Adult teacher-training programmes | 18+ | Not knowingly enrolled. If we discover a child has registered without parental consent, we close the account and delete the data. |
| School / partner programmes for minors | Specific to the programme (e.g., 14–18, 10–18) | Permitted **only with verifiable parental or guardian consent**, mediated by the partner centre or school |
| Application-stage forms | Open | A child may submit an application only with a parent / guardian co-signing the application |

## 2. What we collect from children

We collect the **minimum** data necessary to deliver the programme. For an enrolled child, that typically includes:

- Name, date of birth, gender;
- Parent / guardian name, relationship, contact (phone and email);
- Centre / school the child is enrolled through;
- The child's academic data (lesson progress, attendance, examination scores, certificates);
- Live-class attendance and recordings.

We do **not** collect from children:

- Aadhaar number, passport number, or other government IDs unless the partner programme genuinely requires them and the parent has explicitly consented;
- Health data beyond what a parent self-discloses for accommodation;
- Behavioural / advertising / profiling data — never, in any circumstance.

## 3. Verifiable parental consent

Before processing a child's personal data we obtain **verifiable consent** from the parent or legal guardian. Acceptable means of verification include:

- A **signed paper consent form** ([parental-consent-form.md](parental-consent-form.md)) collected by the partner centre, retained for the duration of enrolment plus the retention window;
- A **government-issued ID** of the parent (e.g., Aadhaar, voter ID, passport) verified by the centre at the point of enrolment — only the verification fact and the type of ID are recorded; the ID number itself is **not** retained;
- An **OTP-verified parental phone number** combined with the signed consent form;
- A **video call** with the parent where the centre verifies identity by visual inspection of the ID (recorded with the parent's permission), only used where in-person verification is impractical.

Consent must be **specific** (the parent is told exactly what data is collected and why), **informed** (the parent is given this notice in plain language and a language they understand), and **freely given**. Consent can be withdrawn at any time by writing to {{DPO_EMAIL}} or contacting the centre.

## 4. What we will not do

In line with DPDP §9 we shall **not**:

- Undertake any **tracking, profiling, or behavioural monitoring** of children;
- Direct **targeted advertising** at children;
- Process a child's personal data in any way that is **likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being** of the child.

## 5. Education through children's accounts

Partner centres and schools may operate enrolments on behalf of children. In those cases:

- The centre is bound by a written **Data Processing Agreement** that requires the same protections as TTII applies directly;
- The centre must keep parental consent forms and produce them on request;
- The centre is not permitted to use the data for its own marketing, fundraising, or any purpose beyond delivering the programme.

## 6. Live classes and recordings

Where a live class involves minors:

- The trainer is informed in advance that minors are present and is briefed on conduct expectations;
- Recording is enabled by default for academic-integrity purposes; the parent / guardian, at the point of consent, is told that recordings are made and how long they are retained;
- Recordings are stored privately and made available only to enrolled learners and authorised TTII / centre staff;
- Recordings are not used to train any AI model, and they are not shared with third-party advertising or analytics services.

## 7. Sharing children's data

We share a child's data only with:

- The partner centre / school the child is enrolled through;
- Service providers strictly necessary to deliver the programme (hosting, video, payment, email) — every one of whom is bound by a DPA;
- A co-branded certification partner, where required to issue the child's certificate (limited to name and programme details);
- Law enforcement, where there is a lawful obligation to disclose.

We do **not** sell, rent, or otherwise share a child's data with advertising networks or data brokers.

## 8. Parents' rights

A parent or legal guardian may, on behalf of the child:

- **Access** the child's personal data and a summary of how it is processed;
- **Correct** inaccurate data;
- **Withdraw consent**, which results in deletion of the child's personal data subject to retention obligations under §10 of this notice;
- **Erase** the child's personal data, subject to the same retention obligations;
- **Lodge a grievance** under the [Grievance Redressal Policy](grievance-redressal-policy.md).

To exercise these rights, write to {{DPO_EMAIL}} from the parent's registered email address, or use the [DPDP Rights Request Form](dpdp-rights-request-form.md).

## 9. The child's own rights

A mature minor (broadly, a child capable of understanding the request) may also raise concerns directly with the Grievance Officer. We will respond to the child sensitively and, depending on the nature of the request, involve the parent or guardian.

## 11. Reporting concerns about a child

If you believe a child is at risk on the Platform — for example, you suspect inappropriate contact with an adult, sexual harassment, exposure to harmful content, or self-harm signals — contact the Grievance Officer ({{GRIEVANCE_OFFICER_EMAIL}}) **and** the Internal Committee under our [POSH Policy](../compliance/posh-policy.md). Where the law requires, we will report the matter to **Childline (1098)** or the local police, including under the **POCSO Act 2012**.

## 12. Updates

We will update this notice when our processing of children's data changes. Material changes are communicated to enrolled parents by email.
